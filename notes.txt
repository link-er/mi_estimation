Overall Remarks:
- In MINE paper: interesting experiments in 4.2. Capturing non-linear dependencies: they ensure that MI(X;f(X)+eps) is invariant across different bijective functions f
- Positive/negative sampling: for each input X we generate 3 « views » of Y, those views are not really used in contrastive method (where the negative samples are the other Y of the batch)



Observations from experiments:
InfoNCE:
- representation normalization is necessary (otherwise dot product scale with hidden dimension and the logsum is super sharp -> vanishing or exploding gradient)
- In theory: Estimated I(X,Z) > log(B), so in high dimension the batch size should be high, in practice any batch size gives more or less the same result
- Noise_Samples > 1 doesn’t change much (still a good way to generate more datapoints from fewer X) 

CLUB:
- Too many training samples makes noise consistency decreasing very sharply (on the other hand infoNCE is super consistent across number of samples)
 (see CLUB/noise_level_dim_32_per_num_samples)
